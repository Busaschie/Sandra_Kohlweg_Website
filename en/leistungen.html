Direct search

Headhunting and direct search for companies in Austria and Germany

Companies don’t usually call me because everything is going wonderfully. Okay – some do, simply because they enjoy a chat with me, but that’s not the rule.

They call me because they want to replace someone who mustn’t know yet. And they call me because finding the right employee really isn’t easy. I support companies in Austria and Germany in searching for and directly approaching specialists and managers. One focus of my work is Carinthia, Styria and Salzburg and, within that, the industrial and technical sector.

‘One focus of my work is the industrial and technical sector in Carinthia, Styria and Salzburg.’

— Kohlweg Headhunting

What kind of person are you actually looking for?

Once I’ve listened to what is needed, one of my most important questions is always: What kind of person would fit in with you? What are you like? What challenges do you bring?

It’s not just about qualifications, work experience or the right keywords in a CV. Especially with specialists and managers, the person also has to fit the company, the role and the people they will be working with.

The right people aren’t looking

This is where my sales background helps. All my working life I had to identify the right companies: find out who needs the product, why they should prefer ours, where the benefits lie for them – and then offer it proactively. Now it’s no different.

Very, very rarely are the right people looking themselves at that moment. The truly well-suited candidate usually works for another company, is very successful there, but is perhaps – hopefully – a little dissatisfied, or at least open.

The key difference between a job advert and direct search

I don’t wait for someone to apply. I search specifically for people who could fit the position and approach them. By now I receive a great many recommendations from my network: since pleasingly many people enjoy working with me, they pass these positive experiences on.

💡 Long-term network: I stay in touch with many high potentials and top performers for years, until one day we find the perfect position.

Headhunting in Carinthia – sometimes the market is simply empty

Of course I also turn projects down. I don’t accept every assignment. In some fields I simply have no access. Or I know the market is hopelessly empty – and then even I can only find people if the company is prepared to compromise.

Having worked in Germany for almost 25 years, I also have very good market access there. That’s why I don’t search only regionally for Austrian companies. Depending on the position, I approach candidates throughout Austria and Germany.

‘And surprisingly, it is often much easier to get someone from Germany excited about Austria than to get someone in Carinthia to move from one valley to the next. As soon as I see they’re in the volunteer fire brigade, my hopes always drop rapidly.’

Why hire a headhunter rather than simply searching yourself?

1. Searching is a full-time job

I search full-time. That means I usually spend weeks on a single position: using my network, holding countless conversations, discarding and searching again. If you have that time in-house, you don’t need me. But usually you simply don’t.

2. Absolute discretion & protection

Who wants to make enquiries at a competitor? It always makes for juicy gossip. As a headhunter, I only reveal my client’s name once there are clear signs of interest in a move. That’s how I protect your company’s reputation.

Carinthia focus

Headhunter in Carinthia – finding employees who really want to come to Carinthia

It is sometimes easier to get someone from Germany excited about a job in Carinthia than to persuade a Carinthian to move from one valley to the next. No joke.

People have roots. They have family, friends, a house, a club and a life that doesn’t simply move with them just because an interesting company 40 kilometres away is offering a good job. And that is exactly what sometimes makes headhunting in Carinthia rather special. Surprisingly, many Germans are very open to relocating – perhaps because distance isn’t such an issue in Germany. It is simply a bigger country.

Why is it difficult to find skilled staff in Carinthia?

The candidate market is limited, and for specialised experts and managers it is even smaller. Particularly in industry, mechanical engineering and technical fields, companies often compete for the same people.

A job advert mainly reaches those who are actively looking for a new job. The people I’m looking for often aren’t. After all, they have a job. They are successful there and would probably stay for the next ten years – unless someone calls them and tells them about an alternative.

How do I find employees for companies in Carinthia?

I don’t wait for the right candidate to apply at some point. I search. I identify companies where people with the required experience might work. I use my network, recommendations and direct approach.

My sales background helps me here: for almost 30 years I did nothing but find out who the person opposite me is, what they need and why my offer might interest them.

Winning candidates from Germany for Carinthia

Having worked in Germany for almost 25 years, I don’t limit my search to Carinthia or Austria. I have very good access to the German market and, for suitable positions, deliberately search there too.

For someone from Hamburg, Munich, Dresden or North Rhine-Westphalia, Carinthia isn’t the next valley. It is a fundamental decision for a new place to live: mountains, lakes, nature, quality of life, housing – combined with salary, career and family.

How is a headhunter’s fee calculated?

The annual gross salary is everything the employee earns within one year at 100% target achievement. This includes the basic salary as well as agreed variable pay components and allowances.

Maximum amount (cap) for very well-paid positions

For particularly highly paid positions, I agree a maximum amount for my fee. After all, my work doesn’t automatically become better or more demanding just because a candidate earns a lot or negotiates particularly well. If the commission approaches €50,000 or more, we therefore agree an individual cap. Live and let live.

What happens if the candidate resigns during the probationary month?

If the placed employee terminates the employment of their own accord within the probationary month, I offer a free replacement search.

Why can I offer headhunting for 15–20%?

These days I live mainly in Transylvania and run my own business. I have no large administrative apparatus and no prestigious offices that my clients have to help pay for. That’s why I can work at a rate I consider much fairer to the market.

That doesn’t mean I put less work into a search. On the contrary: I search myself. I approach the candidates myself. I conduct the interviews myself. I look after my clients myself. Hard work, ambition, skill, a good network and a pretty keen understanding of people – that’s all I bring. And in my view, that’s all that needs paying for.
