Headhunting FAQ – frequently asked questions

Here I answer the questions companies ask me most often about headhunting, direct search and personnel placement.

Process & duration Fees & costs Confidentiality Regions & positions
How long does headhunting take?

Among other things, it depends on how quickly and decisively the company acts. I’ve had everything from under a week to six months.

Sometimes I find the right person very quickly. Other positions take many conversations, recommendations and several attempts.

What really makes a big difference: how quickly does the company respond when an interesting candidate comes along? Good candidates won’t necessarily wait weeks for a decision.

When is a headhunter paid?

With me, the fee only becomes due once the candidate has signed their employment contract.

As a rule, I work on a success basis.

What happens if no candidate is found?

No candidate, no invoice from me.

However, if I realise that a position cannot be filled under the agreed conditions, I reserve the right to end the search.

If the company would still like me to continue, we can agree an advance payment for the further search.

Can a headhunter approach a competitor’s employees?

Of course. After all, that is often exactly where the people with the required experience are.

Sometimes, however, my clients give me a list of so-called off-limits companies. These might be companies they have close or friendly ties with, or that they work for themselves. Naturally, I then don’t approach anyone there.

Can a search be carried out confidentially?

Absolutely. Confidentiality is particularly important when a position is to be refilled and the current post-holder doesn’t know yet.

That’s why I don’t necessarily name my client at the first contact. But I don’t leave a candidate in the dark for ever either. At the latest when they send me their CV and we discuss the position in concrete terms, they find out who their potential employer is.

After all, confidentiality works both ways.

Does the current post-holder need to know about the search?

No.

I once even had a case where the post-holder contacted me, independently of my assignment, because he wanted to be placed elsewhere too. Clearly neither side was entirely happy any more.

How are candidates approached?

Personally and directly.

I identify people whose experience could fit the position and get in touch with them. Initially, it’s not about forcing a new job on anyone.

I want to find out whether the person is open to a change in principle and what would need to happen for a move to be of interest at all.

A great many of the people I approach aren’t looking for a new job at the moment – but they can be won over. And that is exactly what headhunting is about.

What is the difference between headhunting and personnel placement?

These terms are used in quite different ways nowadays. For my work, the decisive point is active search and direct approach.

I don’t wait for suitable candidates to respond to a job advert. I identify people who could fit the position and approach them directly.

Because the right candidate is very often sitting contentedly – or at least half-contentedly – at another company.

Transparent pricing

How much is a headhunter’s fee?

20% of the annual gross salary for successfully filling a single position.
15% of the annual gross salary per position when at least two positions are commissioned.

The basis of calculation is the income the employee earns within one year at 100% target achievement. For particularly highly paid positions, I agree a maximum amount.

With me, there are no additional search or administration costs.

What happens if the candidate doesn’t start the job?

Then I search again.

If a candidate I have placed doesn’t take up the agreed position, I don’t leave my client on their own. I resume the search and look for a replacement free of charge.

What happens if the candidate resigns during the probationary month?

If the placed employee terminates the employment of their own accord within the probationary month, I offer a free replacement search.

Can an Austrian company search for candidates in Germany?

Of course. And for Austrian companies in particular, it can make a great deal of sense.

I worked in Germany for almost 25 years, so for my Austrian clients I deliberately search the German market as well.

Surprisingly, it is sometimes easier to get someone from Germany excited about moving to Austria than to get someone in Carinthia to move from one valley to the next.

Does a headhunter only search in Austria?

No.

I search wherever I can find suitable people – in Austria and Germany, but depending on the position in other countries too.

Overall, we have too few skilled workers. So I’m not particularly keen on the idea of Austrian companies constantly poaching the same employees from one another – and pushing salaries ever higher in the process.

Especially for positions where perfect German is not essential, an international search opens up a considerably larger candidate market.

Which positions can a headhunter fill?

Basically any position whose candidate market they have reasonable access to. And that is exactly why I don’t claim to be equally good at everything.

Blue-collar roles, for example, are not my field. I once failed miserably with tax advisers. And I find site managers difficult too, simply because I know too little about that market.

On the other hand, I have already filled a wide variety of specialist and management positions – from CFO to programmers, engineers, buyers and project managers, through to sales.

Sales is and will remain my favourite field. Surprisingly, though, many technical positions suit me very well too.

And as soon as very good German is not essential for a position, it becomes particularly interesting for me. Then we can open the search internationally – and suddenly a very small candidate market becomes quite a large one.

Can I engage a headhunter even though the position is still filled?

Of course. That is done regularly.

Perhaps no job advert should be published yet. Perhaps the company wants to explore the market first. Or it isn’t yet clear whether anyone better suited to the position can be found at all.

In such cases, I can start by searching confidentially and approaching suitable candidates without revealing my client’s name straight away.

Do I have to engage a headhunter exclusively?

No. However, I think it makes a lot of sense.

The way a position is presented on the market also determines how a company is perceived externally. If several recruitment consultants contact the same people about the same position at the same time, it quickly looks random. And confidentiality becomes more difficult.

I know a competitor who advertises CFO positions on Willhaben (an Austrian classifieds site) and very generously tells callers which company it is. Unfortunate if the current post-holder happens to call.

So, particularly for confidential or senior positions, it should be quite clear who is leading the search, who is being approached and what information goes out.

What does a headhunter need from the company before the search begins?

The absolute minimum is the professional requirements and a realistic salary range. But the ideal approach is something else.

I want to know:

  • What makes you a good employer – and what makes you challenging?
  • Why did the predecessor leave? Or why were they dismissed?
  • What kind of person is the line manager? How do they lead and communicate?
  • What are the things that might get on a new employee’s nerves?

I am very honest with my candidates and don’t hide the challenges either. If a manager is hot-tempered, a candidate should know. If someone communicates in a very matter-of-fact, direct and unemotional way, that is important information too.

I’m always surprised how calmly candidates react to such things. But some say straight away: no, that’s out of the question for me. Perfect. Then we know before both sides invest a lot of time.

It doesn’t pay to sell a position or a company as better than it is. It might get a candidate to sign the contract. But in the medium term it still won’t fit. And then everyone loses.

Is a headhunter worthwhile for medium-sized companies?

It depends.

If someone in the company has enough time, experience, network and enthusiasm for direct search, you can of course search yourself.

However, finding someone quickly becomes a full-time job. I sometimes spend weeks on a single position – researching, approaching people, holding interviews, discarding candidates and searching again.

And a job advert simply doesn’t reach people who aren’t looking for a job themselves. If those are exactly the people who are of interest for a position, a headhunter can pay off very quickly.
